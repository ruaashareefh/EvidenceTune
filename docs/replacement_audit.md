## 3. Review Methodology

Every replacement example was evaluated against EvidenceTune Annotation Guidelines v0.2.

### 3.1 Review Dimensions

| Dimension | Review question |
|---|---|
| Label correctness | Does the category accurately reflect the evidence? |
| Reference accuracy | Is the reference answer supported by the context? |
| Question quality | Is the question clear, specific, and unambiguous? |
| Evidence grounding | Can the expected answer be justified without external knowledge? |
| False-premise detection | Does the question make an assumption contradicted by the context? |

### 3.2 Review Decisions

**Accept**

The example has a defensible category, accurate reference answer, and sufficiently clear question.

**Flag**

The example requires further investigation because of ambiguity, possible label errors, unsupported inferences, or misleading assumptions.

**Exclude**

The example is unsuitable for the primary benchmark because of confirmed annotation defects, incompatible evaluation requirements, or unresolved ambiguity.

### 3.3 Review Principles

- Preserve original SQuAD records and labels.
- Do not modify annotations to improve model performance.
- Do not automatically trust the source dataset's category assignments.
- Do not use external knowledge to establish answerability.
- Do not classify a question as false-premise without a demonstrable contradiction.
- Distinguish missing evidence from evidence contradicting a question's premise.
- Resolve every flagged example before benchmark approval.
- Record decisions and supporting evidence.
- Audit replacement examples before accepting them.

## 7. Key Findings

### Finding 1: False-Premise Questions Require Separate Evaluation

Replacements 6, 7, and 10 contain assumptions explicitly contradicted by their contexts.

| Replacement | Incorrect premise | Evidence-supported correction |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Three customaries | The passage identifies two |
| 7 | Research center located in Woods Hole | The passage identifies Chicago's campus |
| 10 | Three forms of environmental determinism | The passage identifies two |

These examples are potentially valuable for measuring premise detection and correction.

However, they do not fit the primary SQuAD-derived binary evaluation behavior without updated reference answers and scoring rules.

They should be retained as potential challenge-set examples rather than treated as automatically valid unanswerable records.

### Finding 2: Potentially Answerable Questions Labeled Unanswerable

Replacements 2 and 8 contain information that may directly answer their questions.

**Replacement 2:** The passage identifies a strictly negative correlation for two fermions.

**Replacement 8:** The passage describes packet routing under connection-oriented transmission.

Their original generic abstention references may penalize evidence-grounded answers.

Both examples require adjudication.

### Finding 3: Ambiguous Question Construction

Replacement 5 requests unclear information about job types and experience levels.

The passage contains relevant salary findings, but the question does not specify which relationship or result is requested.

Poor question construction can make a benchmark unreliable even when its original answerability label is defensible.

### Finding 4: Valid Evidence-Insufficiency Examples

Replacements 4 and 9 illustrate defensible abstention cases.

Replacement 4 requests alternative terminology not established by the passage.

Replacement 9 asks about companies headquartered in Los Angeles, while the passage only establishes headquarters within Southern California.

These examples test whether the model distinguishes evidence-supported information from information requiring outside knowledge.

### Finding 5: Reference-Answer Equivalence

Replacement 1 uses "Feynman" as its reference answer, while the context identifies "Feynman diagrams."

An evidence-grounded model may reasonably produce the complete term.

This illustrates the need for semantic equivalence in answer grading.

Exact string matching alone may produce misleading evaluation results.

## 9. Replacement and Versioning Plan

If additional candidate exclusions are approved, EvidenceTune must regenerate replacement examples through a reproducible process.

### 9.1 Preservation Requirements

The following must remain unchanged:

- The original source dataset and labels.
- The original development dataset.
- The existing `curated_v1` dataset.
- Previously accepted development examples.
- The held-out test dataset and its checksum.

### 9.2 Curation Configuration

Newly excluded replacement IDs should be added to a versioned exclusion configuration with documented reasons.

The configuration must distinguish between:

- Original baseline exclusions.
- Exclusions discovered during replacement audits.
- False-premise challenge-set candidates.
- Records accepted for continued retention.

### 9.3 Successive Curated Versions

The current curated dataset is:

`data/eval/curated_v1/`

A subsequent candidate dataset should be generated in a new directory, such as:

`data/eval/curated_v2/`

The new manifest should record:

- Parent curated dataset version.
- Source dataset revision.
- Guideline version.
- Exclusion configuration checksum.
- Previously accepted record IDs.
- Newly selected replacement IDs.
- Sampling configuration.
- Development and test artifact checksums.
- Review status.

**Do not overwrite `curated_v1`.**

### 9.4 Required Builder Improvement

The current curation builder only accepts excluded IDs that belong to the original baseline development dataset.

Replacement candidates introduced in `curated_v1` are not members of that original baseline.

Therefore, before generating `curated_v2`, the builder must be extended to support successive curation versions.

It must:

1. Accept the previous curated dataset as an input.
2. Verify the parent manifest and artifact checksums.
3. Preserve accepted records from previous versions.
4. Apply cumulative approved exclusions.
5. Select new candidates deterministically.
6. Block previously excluded and previously rejected records.
7. Preserve the held-out test dataset byte-for-byte.
8. Prevent cross-split source leakage and duplicate records.
9. Record lineage and provenance in the new manifest.
10. Mark newly generated examples as pending semantic review.

This functionality must be tested before use with the real benchmark.

### 9.5 Replacement Review Requirement

New candidates cannot automatically inherit approval.

Every new replacement must undergo the same semantic review process used in this audit.
